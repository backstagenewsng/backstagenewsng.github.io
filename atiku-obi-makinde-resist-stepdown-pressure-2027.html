Atiku, Obi, Makinde Resist Pressure to Step Down for Consensus Candidate

news

By Staff Reporter · September 9, 2026

The presidential candidates of the ADC, NDC and APM have rejected calls by the G-100 coalition to produce a single opposition consensus candidate for the 2027 election, insisting they will each contest independently.

The presidential candidates of three major opposition parties have rejected calls to step down in favour of a consensus candidate for the January 2027 presidential election, dealing a blow to the G-100 coalition's unity project.

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar (ADC), former Anambra Governor Peter Obi (NDC) and Oyo Governor Seyi Makinde (APM) have each insisted they will remain in the race, arguing that their respective platforms represent the best chance of unseating President Bola Tinubu. The G-100, a group of prominent Nigerians pushing for opposition unity, had urged two of the three candidates to step aside to prevent a split of the anti-Tinubu vote.

Political analysts say the failure to produce a consensus candidate mirrors the 2023 election, where Atiku and Obi split the opposition vote and helped Tinubu win with just 37 percent of the total votes cast. With 19 presidential candidates now confirmed by INEC, the 2027 contest is shaping up to be equally fragmented.
