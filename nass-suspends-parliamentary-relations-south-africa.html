National Assembly Suspends Parliamentary Relations With South Africa Over Xenophobic Attacks

news

By Staff Reporter · September 12, 2026

The Nigerian National Assembly has ordered the suspension of parliamentary relations with South Africa in response to persistent xenophobic attacks on Nigerians and other African nationals living in the country.

The Nigerian National Assembly has ordered the immediate suspension of parliamentary relations with South Africa in response to persistent xenophobic attacks on Nigerians and other African nationals living in the country.

The resolution followed a motion brought to the floor of the Senate and supported by lawmakers across party lines, who cited a pattern of violence, discrimination and extrajudicial killings targeting Nigerians in South Africa over the past decade. Former minister Femi Fani-Kayode earlier raised alarm that over 100 Nigerians had been murdered in South Africa within the last five years alone.

Senate President Godswill Akpabio described the situation as unacceptable and directed the Committee on Foreign Affairs to liaise with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to explore further diplomatic actions, including a possible review of trade agreements. The House of Representatives passed a similar resolution, with lawmakers calling on the Federal Government to summon the South African High Commissioner for formal consultations.
